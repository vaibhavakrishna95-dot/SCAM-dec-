Scam Awareness & Safety Guide

Social engineering tactics evolve constantly. Learn to spot the underlying deception mechanics behind phishing, fake job offers, OTP theft, and payment fraud.

Phishing & Brand Impersonation

Attackers mimic recognizable brands (banks, postal couriers, streaming services) claiming your account is locked or a delivery is held.

"Your Netflix subscription has expired. Update billing details immediately to avoid cancellation."
Defense Action:

Never tap embedded links. Navigate independently to the service's official website or app to check your account status.

OTP & 2FA Theft

Scammers pose as bank executives, telecom agents, or tech support, claiming an urgent security glitch or SIM upgrade requires your OTP.

"SBI Alert: Unusual login from Russia detected. Share the 6-digit OTP sent to your phone to cancel the transfer."
Defense Action:

Banks and security desks will NEVER request your OTP or PIN over phone, SMS, or email. Treat all OTP requests as hostile.

Fake Jobs & "Task" Fraud

Unsolicited offers on WhatsApp or Telegram promising high daily returns for simple tasks (liking YouTube videos, reviewing hotels).

"Earn $300-$800/day part-time from home. Just like 5 videos daily. Contact manager on Telegram @EarnQuick."
Defense Action:

Legitimate employers do not conduct hiring exclusively over anonymous messaging apps or ask you to pay "advance task deposits".

Lottery & Fake Prize Fraud

Claims that your mobile number or email was randomly selected to win cash, a luxury vehicle, or shopping vouchers.

"CONGRATULATIONS! Your mobile number won £750,000 in the UK National Lottery. Call +44... to claim."
Defense Action:

You cannot win a lottery you never bought a ticket for. Any prize requiring a "processing fee" or "customs tax" is a scam.

QR Code & Payment Trap

Buyers on marketplace apps claiming they want to purchase your item, sending a QR code telling you to "scan to receive payment".

"I have initiated payment for your couch. Scan this QR code and enter your UPI PIN to accept money."
Defense Action:

Golden Rule: Entering your PIN or scanning a merchant QR code ALWAYS deducts money from your account, never receives it.

Shortened & Spoofed Links

Links using URL shorteners (bit.ly, tinyurl) or lookalike domains (e.g. paypa1-security.xyz) to conceal malicious payloads.

"Your FedEx parcel is delayed due to incorrect address. Confirm now at http://bit.ly/3xX-parcel-fedx"
Defense Action:

Use ScamShield's text-based URL inspector to check suspicious link patterns without ever visiting the destination.

Emergency Incident Response Checklist

If you accidentally clicked a suspicious link, shared credentials, or authorized an unfamiliar transfer, follow this immediate triage procedure:

STEP 1 Freeze Cards & Accounts

Immediately use your banking app to freeze affected credit/debit cards and lock netbanking.

STEP 2 Change Passwords

Change passwords from a secure separate device. Invalidate active login sessions.

STEP 3 Revoke 2FA Devices

Check for unauthorized secondary devices added to your Google, Apple, or email accounts.

STEP 4 Report to Cyber Authorities

File an incident report on your national cybercrime reporting portal with screenshots.
